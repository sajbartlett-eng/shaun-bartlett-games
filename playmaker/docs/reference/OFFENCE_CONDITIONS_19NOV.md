# The offence's conditions (Shaun, 19 Nov 2026)

Each item is a way to create an advantage under the rules. It is written as something the coverage map can measure: who is involved, what must be true, and what it pays. These are building blocks the computer combines and rules in or out. They are never plays to run (D-156). The defence mirrors every item: it reads each one forming and works to deny it, within the list rules.

## A. Posting and sealing

1. **Post-up with the defender behind,** basket side: back to the basket, entry over the top, then his post move against the defender's post contest.
2. **Defender fronting, attacker basket side:** wait for a swing pass that changes the angle, then the entry or the lob over the front.
3. **Three-quarter denial:** step through or reposition to the open side, then the entry.
4. **Duck-in:** step into the lane as the ball arrives, sealing the defender on your back.
5. **Seal on the switch:** a big pins the smaller defender a screen left on him.
6. **High-post flash:** cut to the elbow or high post to receive, then face up, pass or drive.
7. **Dunker spot or short corner:** sit behind the help, ready for the dump-off or the lob.
8. **Post-to-post cross screen:** one big screens for the other to free a deep seal.

## B. Off the ball: moving the defence without the ball

9. **Drag a defender:** reposition so his defender has to follow, opening a gap in the zone of influence where he was helping.

   9b. **Off-ball seal:** a defender close to a passing lane usually has one arrow on the receiver and the other angled 45° towards the passer. A teammate seals him from the side that makes his lane arrow the one pointing diagonally round the screener. The screen rule then blocks that arrow, so he can't jump the lane, and the pass is safe unless he wins a Challenge. The pass mustn't travel through his own square. No rule change: it is the screen rule (Shaun, 19 Nov).
10. **Open the passing angle:** step off the line of a defender's arrow, or out of his reach, so the pass to you can't be intercepted.
11. **V-cut:** move in with one of Move or Quick Step, then back out with the other once the defender reacts. You keep your spot and wear out his influence. It can be done with two Moves over two phases, but only when the main action is on the other side, so the lanes don't clog.
12. **L-cut:** come up the lane line, then out to the wing, to get open on the catch.
13. **Basket cut:** the defender is watching the ball, so cut to the rim.
14. **Backdoor:** the defender is denying the pass, so cut behind him.
15. **Flash cut:** into an open gap in the middle as the defence shifts.
16. **Drift or lift on a drive:** move to the corner or the wing along the help chain, so the kick-out has a target.
17. **Relocate after passing:** pass, then move to the open spot.
18. **Space and occupy:** hold a spot that pins a helper's attention (attention without the ball).
19. **Clear out:** empty a side to take help away from a matchup.

   19b. **Isolation by spacing (Shaun):** deliberately move one or more attackers, and the threats they carry, away from a highly advantageous position and matchup, so their defenders are drawn away with them. The favoured matchup is then played with help too far to arrive in time. It works when the players moved away still carry enough threat that their defenders can't leave them.
   19c. **Dribble at, to force the backdoor:** the ball handler dribbles toward a teammate's defender, pushing him up the floor, so the teammate cuts behind him.

## C. Screens

20. **On-ball screen:** ball handler drive or pull-up. The screener then rolls, short-rolls, pops, or slips before contact if the defence overplays.
21. **Reject the screen:** the defender cheats over, so go the other way.
22. **Re-screen or flip the angle:** the defender went under or around, so set it again from the other side.
23. **Off-ball screens:** down screen or pin-down; flare (away from the ball); back screen (the lob, or the basket cut); screen away from the ball.
24. **Curl, fade or straight cut off a screen,** reading the defender's chase.
25. **Stagger:** two screens in sequence for one shooter.
26. **Screen the screener:** a second screen for the man who just screened.
27. **Back screen on the roller's defender** during a pick and roll.
28. **Handoff:** a short pass with a body in the way.
29. **Hold or pin:** the screener stays put to keep a defender out of the play, within the screen's rules.

## D. With the ball

30. **Beat him off the dribble:** Dribble against Agility, into a lane or space.
31. **Outrun him:** reach a square his defender can't get to set before the shot.
32. **Attack the closeout:** a rushed defender is easier to drive past or shoot over.
33. **Hesitate:** freeze the defence so it commits first.
34. **Retreat dribble:** against a trap or hedge, back out for space and a passing angle.
35. **Probe:** move the ball to shift the defence, then pass to what it opens.
36. **Shoot over a weak contest:** a good shooter against a poor contest rating.
37. **Punish the sag:** take the shot the drop coverage gives.
38. **Create separation:** a controlled Move away after the defender commits, then the set shot.

## E. Passing

39. **Swing:** move the ball faster than the defence can shift.
40. **Skip:** the far side, against a long closeout.
41. **Extra pass:** give up a good shot for a better one.
42. **Entry angle:** pass to a teammate who has a better angle into the post.
43. **Pocket pass or lob:** to the roller.
44. **Hit ahead:** to a teammate the defence hasn't caught up with.

## F. The help chain: three or more players

45. **Drive and kick:** the drive pulls a helper, and the pass goes to the man he left.
46. **Drive and dump:** the drive pulls the big, and the pass goes to the big at the rim.

   46b. **Baseline drive to the weak corner:** a baseline drive pulls the help over, and the far-corner player gets the kick-out as the defence rotates.
47. **Cut behind help:** a player dives while his defender commits elsewhere (Shaun's pick-and-roll corner dive).
48. **Beat the double-team:** two on the ball leaves four on three.
49. **Overload a side:** more attackers than defenders can cover on one side.

## F2. Against a defence weighted to areas (Shaun, 19 Nov)

The game has no "zone" setting. A defence is more zone-like when its defenders' pull leans toward gaps rather than toward their men, which can be read from the list (step 22b's two responsibilities). These conditions become credible as that lean grows, and drop out when the defence is weighted to men.

49b. **Attack the gap** between two area-weighted defenders, so both must commit and one man is left free.
49c. **Flash to the high post or the short corner,** to receive where two area-weighted defenders' areas meet.
49d. **Overload an area:** more attackers in one defender's area than he can cover, then pass to the one left.
49e. **Skip over the shift:** area-weighted defenders move with the ball, so a quick skip pass beats it to the far side.

## F3. Inbounds (scenario cards)

49f. **Screen to free a receiver** for the inbound pass.
49g. **The inbounder as a threat:** after the pass in, he steps onto the court and becomes a cutter or shooter while his defender watches the ball.
49h. **The lob to the rim** when the receiver's defender fronts or ball-watches.

## G. Hunting matchups

50. **Force a switch** to get the weak defender onto the target.
51. **Attack the helper:** target the defender whose job is to help.
52. **Attack a foul-prone defender:** fouls are +5 plus free throws.
53. **Attack a tired or locked defender:** one already rushed or locked this phase.

## H. What the offence must manage

54. **The closely guarded count** before it reaches −5.
55. **Three seconds:** move out of the key before two phase ends.
56. **Don't clog the lanes:** off-ball movement must not block the main action's drive or passing lanes.
57. **The budget:** one Move per player per phase; two Quick Steps per side; two passes, two screens, two dribbles, one Hesitate and one shot per phase.

## Ruling out what isn't credible (from the scout and the rules)

- **The players can't do it:** the shot it creates isn't worth more than the bar for that player, or a roll it needs (Dribble, Pass, Screen) has too small a chance.
- **There isn't time:** the players can't reach the squares it needs in the phases left.
- **There aren't enough tokens:** it needs more Moves, Quick Steps or screens than the phase has.
- **It clogs the main action:** it would block the lanes the main play needs.
- **The defence already shuts it:** against the predicted defence it creates nothing.

What's left is ranked by value at its real chance.

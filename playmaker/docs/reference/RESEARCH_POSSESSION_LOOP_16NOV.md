# Research: scouting and the possession-planning loop (16 Nov 2026)

A summary of what the research found that bears on the coverage map and on the goals. These are reference material, not rules: answers must still fall out of the cards and the rules (D-156). FIBA and European sources come first; NBA sources are marked.

## The loop coaches run

The loop runs scout → ranked goals → matchup and advantage map → actions chosen by condition → setup → read and react inside the possession → back into the scout.
- **The plan is three or four keys.** FIBA's coaching manuals (Levels 2 and 3) present the scout as "what your team will do", in three or four key points. Messina reports the Spurs asked for "one page, the three things we had to do in order to win" (NBA).
- **Advantage states inside a possession** (McCormick's SABA model; Canada Basketball's explode, explore, execute):

  | State | What it looks like | What the offence does |
  |---|---|---|
  | Disadvantage | Trap, denial, a dead ball | Escape |
  | Neutral | The defence is set | Run an action to create an advantage |
  | Small advantage | Late, off balance, closing out, a switch | Keep the defence rotating until it becomes big |
  | Big advantage | An open layup, or a catch-and-shoot three for a good shooter | Take it |

  The defence pushes the state back to neutral.
- **Advantage is player-relative** (Franz Nanni): the same closeout can be neutral against one player and an advantage for another. This is why the map's values come from the cards.

## FIBA rule effects that shape coverage

- **There is no defensive three-second rule in FIBA** (Art. 26 is offensive only). Help defenders can live in the paint. Packing the paint is "much more feasible" than in the NBA (*Gigantes del Basket*); Dončić after EuroBasket 2022: "here in Europe, the team defense is more impressive... you don't have the three-second violation."
- **The consequence:**
  - defenders of non-shooters can sit in the lane;
  - each non-shooter on the offence costs more;
  - the offence wins by making the helper's own man a threat (shooters in the corners, a big in the dunker spot, cutters behind the helper).

## Coverage principles that should fall out of the map (checks, not instructions)

- **Help comes from whoever guards the least dangerous attacker who can get there.** Never from an elite shooter's defender. FIBA Level 2 picks the rotation by which attacker is the bigger threat, and lets the coach name who rotates to an outstanding shooter.
- **Ranking threats:** the most dangerous attacker closest to scoring comes first.
- **What to give up:** a defence can't take away everything. It ranks the opponent's options for the players on the floor, takes away the top one or two, and concedes the cheapest.
- **Coverage by attacker type:**

  | The attacker | His defender |
  |---|---|
  | Elite shooter | Stays attached |
  | Driver who can't shoot | Gets space and walls the drive |
  | Post scorer | Is fronted or helped early |
  | Weak ball handler | Is made to handle the ball |

- **Closeouts:** short and controlled on a driver, high on a shooter, to his strong-hand side.
- **Hunt the helper.** The offence moves its players so the weakest defender becomes the helper, then attacks his decision. In FIBA this is often worth more than beating the man on the ball.

## Offence: wearing the defence down

- **Space before advantage** (Basketball Australia). Every starting spot should force its defender to choose between his man and help.
- **Create, keep, pass on, convert, or reset:**
  - attack an advantage at once (the 0.5-second decision);
  - when help comes, pass to the player the helper left ("dominoes", "kick and swing");
  - move the ball ahead rather than behind, which gives a longer closeout (Messina);
  - change spacing while an action develops (Messina).
- **The shot clock as a goal switcher** (Basketball Australia, the three Ps): Pace (early advantage), then Poise (create space, move the ball and bodies), then Penetration (a paint touch to score or collapse the defence).

## Feedback

- **Live scouting checks the pre-game picture.** Elite coaches adjust mostly before problems arrive (73% of adjustments proactive; *Scientific Reports*, 2026).
- **Avoid overreacting.** Change a goal only after repeated evidence, or one large signal.
- **The first possession after a timeout scores best,** and the second drops as the defence adjusts (EuroLeague 2023–24, 2,773 timeouts). A fresh plan has a short shelf life.
- **Counter-ladder:** plan, then their likely counter, then our counter to that. The computer only needs the next rung.

## Formal models

- **Expected Possession Value** (Cervone, D'Amour, Bornn, Goldsberry; NBA tracking) values every moment of a possession, so each pass, drive or shot is scored by how much it changes the value. The coverage map is the same idea, built from the rules.
- **Ecological dynamics** (Araújo, Davids, Passos) treats the attacker, defender and basket as one system, where distance and angle decide when the attacker breaks it.

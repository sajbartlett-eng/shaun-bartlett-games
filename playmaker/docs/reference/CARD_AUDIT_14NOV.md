# Card audit, 14 November 2026: the evidence behind D-184

Senior professional level, FIBA rules and the FIBA three-point line. Ratings are on a 1-to-20 scale, 5% a point. An offensive rating is the chance an open shot goes in, with no defender within reach. A contest rating is taken off the shot: a soft contest (an adjacent defender with a hand up) takes off the contest rating minus 3; a hard contest takes off the full rating.

## What was faithful (kept)

- **Layups and dunks for finishers.** Truly open, controlled finishes by professionals go in about 90 to 95% of the time, and open dunks about 93 to 97%. League rim percentages (about 64% in Europe, 67 to 70% in the NBA) are lower only because most rim shots are contested.
- **Free throws.** European leagues about 76%, the NBA about 78%. Positions and specialists match the cards.
- **Floaters and post shots.** They are almost always contested. Through the contest rules the cards give 55 to 70%, the same as the best real floater and post scorers (Brunson 54 to 55% on floaters; Embiid 54 to 59% on post-ups).
- **Soft contests at the rim.**

## What was too high (changed)

- **Open threes for good shooters.** EuroLeague set-feet catch-and-shoot threes go in at about 39%, and about 31% off the dribble. Unguarded EuroLeague spot-up threes go in at 43%, guarded ones at 33%. An elite FIBA catch-and-shoot season is about 50% (Rodrigue Beaubois 52%). The NBA's best wide-open seasons are 46 to 57%. The Sharpshooter cards were 55 to 65%.
- **Open mid-range and long twos.** European mid-range runs about 39 to 40%. The best mid-range shooters anywhere make 50 to 55% of all their attempts (Durant 55%, NBA). Cards went up to 80%.
- **Hard contests on jump shots.** Real tracking: on threes, a very tight contest (0 to 2 ft) gives about 28 to 29%, a tight one (2 to 4 ft) about 29 to 33%, and wide open about 38 to 42%. A hard contest is worth only about 2 to 3 rating points on a three. Defenders' identity changes three-point shooting little.
- **Dunk contests.** Dunks are the shot least affected by a defender (about 14 points from a defender in front, NCAA). The cards gave bigs their largest contests against dunks.
- **The Rim Protector A's hard layup contest.** It left an open layup at 35%, below any season on record. The best seasons are about 41 to 45% at the rim (Hibbert, Wembanyama); an elite rim protector about 50%, an average one about 61%.
- **The 80% open-layup floor** for shooters and stretch bigs was a little low. Players of that type make about 77 to 78% of all rim shots, contested ones included.

## Teaching stake

At the old values, an open three by a good shooter was worth about as much as an open layup. In real basketball the open layup is worth clearly more, about 1.8 points against 1.3 to 1.4. A basketball IQ trainer must not teach the reverse.

## The two new types

- **Slashing Wing.** Common in FIBA leagues below the EuroLeague's top, and present in the EuroLeague.
  - Isaïa Cordinier: 2P 54 to 57%, 3P 28 to 30%, FT 80 to 84%.
  - Chima Moneke: 2P 63%, FT 78% on 5.5 attempts a game.
  - It teaches a distinct defensive lesson: wall up the drive and give him the three.
- **Switch Centre.** About 43% of EuroLeague big-man minutes go to versatile or switching bigs.
  - Joel Bolomboy: 2P 66 to 68%, FT 74 to 81%, called the best switching big in the EuroLeague.
  - Switch bigs hold guards to about league-average shooting on switches, but protect the rim clearly worse than true rim protectors. The NBA gap is 10 to 18 percentage points at the rim (Adebayo against Gobert), so their layup contest is 8 against 10.
- **Not added: the Movement Shooter.** It would need a conditional rule (an exception to the rushed penalty), and Shaun rules out conditionals on cards.

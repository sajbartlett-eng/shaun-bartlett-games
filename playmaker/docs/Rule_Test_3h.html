Step 3h: wedge and positional contests, a short comparison

Four variants, built as engine settings and not adopted. Short budget: one complete solver level per position (10 s budget), 2 seeds; play-outs at the computer's 0.5 s budget.

Findings. None of the three new variants does what the change is for. Contest foul rolls stay rare in every variant: none in A, 2 in 34 possessions in B and C, 1 in D (and-ones 0 or 1 in each). The reason is the reaction: a defender who can still react uses it to reach a Front contest (59% to 67% of shots under B, C and D), and one who can't reach the shooter's line now cannot contest at all, so more shots go up with no eligible contester (21% in A, 26% to 34% in the others). Interceptions roughly double in every new variant, including D with a single arrow (won per possession 0.26 in A, 0.56 to 0.65 in B, C and D): the contest geometry changes how both computers use their arrows, not only the wedge. Card values move little (means 22.7 to 24.1 against 23.4), but card verdicts change on 5 (D), 8 (C) and 9 (B) of the 16 cards at this budget, with both seeds agreeing in every variant. Reactions spent turning on the spot were already rare (5% in A) and disappear in the others. The plan change rate is about the same everywhere (72% to 81%). The play-outs are small (34 possessions a variant), so read the per-possession rates as directions, not as exact figures.
Recommendation. Ranked by the hierarchy. 1. Keep A, the current rules (System Fidelity): no variant makes the foul roll or the and-one appear, and each shifts verdicts on a third to over half of the cards and doubles interceptions, a large change with no gain yet shown. A wrong rule is worse than a slow one. 2. If positional contests are still wanted, test D next, not the wedge (Legibility): it changes the fewest verdicts, keeps one arrow, and B and C show the wedge adds nothing D lacks. 3. The lever that would make Side and Behind appear is the reaction's freedom to turn: a variant where a reaction Move keeps the defender's facing (a Quick Step already does) would test that directly, at some cost to the agency the proposal keeps (Player Agency).
